# Genre playbook

Apply the grammar rules everywhere; this file sets what varies by document type.

## Professional email

- Length: 1–6 sentences. One topic per email; number multiple points ("1. ... 2. ...").
- No pleasantry opener. First contact: "Hi Dr. X, this is <your name>." + one-line purpose. Replies: match the thread's register — if the other side dropped greetings, drop them too.
- State the ask explicitly and once. Deadlines as formal dates ("due August 17").
- Lower the recipient's activation energy: say how small the task is, attach what they need, name the exact email subject/sender they should look for.
- Contractions: acceptable in ongoing casual threads; avoid with language-picky recipients (professors, editors, lawyers).
- Close with "Thank you!" or "Thank you so much for X!" — no "Best regards" except first formal contact ("Best, <first name>" is the standing signature).
- Never ask a referee or reviewer for a specific score; give them material instead.

## Academic paper / thesis

- Everything in grammar-rules.md is mandatory; also read typography-latex.md.
- "We" for authors. Present tense for what the paper does ("Section 5 evaluates...").
- Contributions as a numbered, parallel list ("The contribution of this paper is twofold. First, ... Second, ...").
- Claims carry evidence in the same sentence or the next. No claim outruns the experiments.
- Terminology: define once, in italics, then use the same term forever — no elegant variation for technical nouns.

## Article / blog post / announcement

- Open with the result or the artifact, not the journey. First paragraph = what exists now and why the reader cares.
- Concrete numbers with sources; every external claim linkable.
- Sections short, headings in sentence case, no more than two heading levels.
- The owner's public-writing voice (X/blog) is plain and artifact-first: show the thing, state one owned opinion, skip marketing adjectives ("powerful", "seamless", "game-changing" banned).

## README / technical docs / reports

- Structure: what it is (one sentence) → how to run it (copy-pasteable) → how it works → limits.
- Imperative mood for instructions ("Run...", "Set..."). Exact paths, exact commands, no "simply".
- State limitations plainly; an honest limits section is a feature.
- Tables for enumerable facts only; prose for reasoning.

## CV / resume

- Verb-first bullets, one accomplishment per bullet, numbers inline, no meta framing ("Benchmark scope at launch (July 2026):" is banned; fold facts into the accomplishment sentence).
- Every date, title, and count must match the authoritative public record (official program listings, offer letters, arXiv). Cross-check before adding.
- Spell out counts of people; numerals for metrics.
- Reverse-chronological everywhere; no entry may contradict another entry's timeline.
